Overview

The league consists of 16 franchises replaying each year of baseball beginning with the 1969 season. Seasons are played over approximately five months using Diamond Mind Baseball, currently version 12a. Standings and box scores are distributed through the league’s regular reports. Before each new season, owners submit keeper lists, the Annual Draft is conducted, and the season begins.

Definitions

Gruntled
A player who has reached the required percentage of real-life playing time and is eligible to be retained for the following season with respect to playing-time requirements.
Disgruntled
A player who has not reached the required percentage of real-life playing time and therefore may not be retained for the following season.
Hobbled
A player whose performance is reduced by Diamond Mind Baseball after the player reaches the applicable playing-time limit.
Keeper
A player retained by a franchise for the following season.
Keeper list
The list of players a franchise retains for the following season.
Annual Draft
The league’s single annual draft from one available-player pool, including debut players, unrostered or released players, and disgruntled players.

League Structure

The 16 franchises are divided into 2 leagues, each with 2 divisions of 4 teams. Each team plays 162 games. The Diamond Mind computer manager manages both home and visiting teams using each owner’s submitted manager profile. League and division assignments remain constant.

Rosters

Each franchise must carry between 32 and 45 players and must have sufficient batting and pitching resources to complete a 162-game schedule. As a planning guide, sufficient batting resources are approximately 600–700 plate appearances per position. Sufficient pitching resources are 162 starts and approximately 300–350 relief appearances. Pitcher usage is measured by batters faced.

Active Roster

A team may carry up to 25 players on its active roster and should ordinarily maintain at least 24 healthy and eligible active players. The active roster must include at least 8 healthy pitchers. Temporary player unavailability does not necessarily require an immediate roster move.

Annual Draft

Annual Draft Player Pool

The Annual Draft uses one player pool. The pool includes eligible debut players, previously unrostered players, released players, and disgruntled players who could not be kept. A player may be drafted only if the player is included in the official current-season Diamond Mind database.

Draft Picks and Roster Limits

Each team receives a number of its own Annual Draft picks equal to 40 minus the number of players on its keeper list. Picks may be traded or acquired. Each team must finish the Annual Draft with a roster of at least 32 and no more than 45 players. A pick that would take a team above 45 players is forfeited if it has not been traded away before it is reached.

Draft Order

The order for the first round is determined at random for the 12 non-playoff teams. The remaining 4 slots are in reverse order of regular season W-L record. All ties are broken by worse head-to-head play, division record, rock-paper-scissors-lizard-Spock, or coin flip—in that order. The drafting order for all subsequent rounds will be in reverse order of regular season W-L record, regardless of postseason status.

Time Allotted

The first round of the draft will be conducted in a “trickle” fashion, i.e., there will be no time limit to make a selection unless the trickle phase exceeds a week, in which case, picks will be subject to a 12-hour time limit.

When the second round commences, 12 hours will be allotted to each pick, though it is hoped that these picks will be made far more quickly. The league will make allowances for the hours typically dedicated to sleep.

From the third round on, unless there are special circumstances (e.g., birth of a child), picks will have a four-hour time limit. If an owner does not pick within four hours and has not notified the commissioner or the draft master, the pick will be skipped, but that owner may jump in with a pick at any time. When “real life” intrudes on the draft, owners are strongly encouraged to submit a list of players and/or selection criteria to both the draft master and the commissioner or someone else in the league who is more available.

Please try to pick quickly. Remember that 15 other people are waiting to make their picks. Just because you have four hours does not mean you must use all four of them. Try to negotiate trades before your pick, not while “on the clock.”

If the draft proceeds to a previously skipped owner without that owner having made the prior pick, an additional four-hour window will not be granted and this turn also will be skipped, again with the right to jump in with picks at the owner's earliest convenience. If the pick in question is that owner’s final pick of the draft, however, that pick is forfeited.

Manager Profiles

All games are played on the commissioner’s computer using manager profiles submitted by each franchise owner. Profile forms and instructions are provided by the commissioner. Owners may set both manager tendencies and player tendencies. Ideally, profiles should be designed to function with minimal changes in the event of injuries.

Player Eligibility and Usage

Position and Role Eligibility

During the regular season, starting pitchers may pitch in relief and relief pitchers may start. Relief pitchers not rated as starters will generally be limited in how long they can remain effective in a start.

Hitters may play only positions for which they have a defensive rating, subject to these exceptions:

  • A center fielder may play any outfield position.
  • A left fielder or right fielder may play the other corner-outfield position.
  • A shortstop may play any infield position.
  • Only infielders, not catchers, may play first base without a first-base rating.
  • Catchers may only catch.

Unrated players may not play a defensive position. Using a player at an unrated position may have negative consequences within the simulation.

Regular-Season Usage Limits

A hitter may accumulate no more than 105% of the plate appearances recorded in the MLB season being simulated. A pitcher may face no more than 105% of the batters faced in the MLB season being simulated.

DMB Playing-Time Limits and Hobbled Players

DMB’s playing-time limits feature will be enabled. Players will perform at full strength until they reach their playing-time limit. When a player exceeds the limit, the player immediately becomes a replacement-level performer (hobbled).

During games, the computer manager (CM) will adjust its strategy to account for performance-level changes for players who have exceeded their limits. The CM will recognize when a player has a replacement-level event table and avoid using the player when appropriate. Specific roles assigned in a team’s manager profile may still influence how the CM handles the player.

Exceeding Usage

Players who reach 105% of their available usage will be dropped from the active roster following the game in which the player reaches or exceeds 105%. The roster spot remains open until the owner fills it. If the owner does not fill the spot and the roster is not legal, the commissioner will call up an eligible player. An overused player is not eligible to play again until the postseason.

Platoon Limits

Platoon limits are eliminated. Instead, the league uses DMB’s Playing Time Limits feature. Once a player exceeds 125% of the specified plate appearances against one side, DMB will hobble that player to replacement level against that side. The player remains unaffected on the non-overused side. The playing-time limits feature applies to all players, including players with 500 or more total plate appearances or 100 or more plate appearances against a particular side.

Once a player exceeds 125% against one side, that player will no longer be required to be removed from the profile on that side. The player may remain active on that side and (probably) just suck. An owner may instruct the commissioner to use individual player tendencies to (attempt to) mitigate any adverse effects of keeping an overused player on the profile, but that should not be necessary as the CM will “know” that the player is now “replacement level” and will (in theory) avoid using him.

Intentionally overplaying a player on one side in order to tank is frowned upon. Since determining intent is difficult and subjective, this policy will be enforced on the honor system.

Owner Responsibility

The franchise owner is solely responsible for monitoring player usage and injuries. Listings of player usage and injuries are included with each daily report. At a bare minimum, read those sections and make appropriate adjustments as necessary.

This is your team, your league. Monitoring your team should be a joy, not a chore. If this league is not enough fun for you to budget five (5) minutes of your day to it, you should give up your team.

Teams Unable to Field All Positions

Any team that is unable to field all positions, maintain 9 healthy pitchers, maintain 2 healthy and eligible catchers, shortstops, and center fielders, or maintain a 24-player active roster will have players assigned from the free-agent pool to fill the void. The commissioner will select restricted free agents based on the most available playing time (PA or BFP) at the position being covered and the absence of a “Prone” injury rating.

Each free agent signed will result in the forfeiture of a draft pick for the following season, starting with the eighth (8th) round pick, if available. If that pick has been traded, that team’s next lower-numbered round available pick will be forfeited. A second playing time violation will result in the loss of the team’s next lower-numbered round pick, etc.

To reduce the possibility of a team chronically running short of playing time in subsequent seasons due to poor planning in the current season, any forfeited picks will be replaced with picks at the end of the draft.

Disgruntled Players and Keeper Eligibility

A player listed on the playing-time report at 75% is eligible to be retained for the following season, provided the player’s career has not ended and all other keeper rules are satisfied. A player who does not reach 75% of real-life playing time is disgruntled and may not be retained. All players below 75% who do not qualify under one of the exceptions below are returned to the Annual Draft player pool. This is a firm rule; an owner’s intent to use a player later in the season does not create an exception.

  • A player on a roster who had zero PA or BFP in the real-life season is automatically eligible to be kept (“auto-gruntled”).
  • An injured player may qualify under the prorated formula below, but a player below 75% must be appealed to the commissioner.
Injury appeal formula
(PA/BFPsim ÷ PA/BFPreal) × (162 ÷ (162 − injury days)) ≥ 75%

A player appealed because of injury must satisfy the formula.

Highlighted rule

AL/NL Variance for Designated Hitters

Because the AL plays 130 games with the designated hitter and the NL plays only 32 games with the designated hitter, each team may retain 1 player below the normal 75% threshold. For that 1 player only, the threshold is 60% for an AL team and 15% for an NL team.

In-season Transactions

During the season, owners may make trades and promote or demote players within their existing rosters. Except when a team runs out of eligible players, only players drafted or otherwise already on league rosters may appear during the season.

Disabled List

An injured active player may be replaced by a reserve player by placing the injured player on the 15-day disabled list.

Player Movement

Frequent movement between the active roster and farm is discouraged. A farmed player must remain farmed for at least 10 days, except that the minimum is waived when an injury requires a move.

Trading

The trading window opens approximately one week before the Annual Draft, after the draft order has been established. The trade deadline is July 31.

Players and draft picks may be traded. Draft picks may be traded only for the current Annual Draft and the following season’s Annual Draft. Trades must be completed in their entirety; performance conditions, players to be named later, and future considerations inside or outside the league are not permitted.

A proposed trade will not be accepted if it would produce an illegal roster. The commissioner will return the proposal to the participating owners for reworking. A trade becomes official when announced by the commissioner.

Because this is a career-based league, dumping is completely and utterly allowed and ethical. No trades will be contested based on assumed dumping.

Postseason

4Division winners qualify
2Rounds
Best of 7All series, 2-3-2

The American League and National League each play a best-of-seven League Championship Series. The league champions meet in a best-of-seven World Series. All postseason series use a 2-3-2 home-field format.

Each of the four division winners will make the post-season. All ties are broken by head-to-head play, overall run differential or coin flip—in that order. The winner of each of those series will play each other in the World Series. There is no wild card.

All three series are seven games, in format, the team with the better regular season record playing games 1, 2, 6 and 7 at home.

Once a batter reaches 10% of real-life plate appearances, or a pitcher reaches 10% of games or games started, the player will be unavailable for future games, or starts, in that series.

Awards

At the conclusion of the World Series, it is both customary and expected for each owner to congratulate the league champion by email. The commissioner must be copied on the email, but an owner may choose to copy as many fellow owners as s/he sees fit.

Following the season, MVP, Cy Young, Fireman, and Manager of the Year awards are voted on by the 16 managers, plus the commissioner if the commissioner is not a manager. Each team votes for 10 players for MVP, 5 pitchers for Cy Young, 3 relief pitchers for Fireman, and 3 managers other than the voter for Manager of the Year.

Award voting is mandatory and ballots are expected to be received in a timely manner. The lottery results for the subsequent season draft will not be announced until all (or at least the non-playoff) teams have turned in their ballots. The penalty for not voting is the deferring of the non-voting team’s draft picks until the ballot is received by the commissioner.

Expansion and Vacancies

Expansion and vacancy drafts are rare and will be configured for the circumstances of the event. Before such a draft, the commissioner will announce the participating franchises, keeper and Protected List rules, available-player pool, draft order, selection limits, and roster targets.

The term Protected List, and the related term unprotected player, apply only to an expansion or vacancy draft.

When multiple franchises are vacant, the commissioner may place eligible keepers from the vacant rosters into a restricted pool available only to incoming owners. After that draft, the incoming owners participate in the regular Annual Draft under the same rules as all other teams.

Picks in an expansion or vacancy draft may not be traded, although players acquired in that draft may later be traded.

League Administration

Commissioner Responsibilities

The commissioner administers league operations, maintains the official league files and records, announces transactions, conducts the Annual Draft, and addresses questions concerning the application of league rules. The commissioner may consult experienced owners or appoint owners to advisory or administrative roles as appropriate. Current officeholders are listed in the league directory.

Commissioner Corrections

The commissioner may correct an administrative, roster, draft, transaction, database, or rules-application error after it is discovered. When practical, the correction should restore the result that would have occurred had the error not been made, while minimizing disruption to subsequent league activity. The commissioner will announce the correction and any required action.

League Files and Website

The commissioner will identify the official location of the league database, constitution, statistics, daily reports, and other league resources.
