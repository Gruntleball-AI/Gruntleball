Monday, April 28, 1975

End of Vietnam War and Fall of Saigon: At 10:24am ICT in Saigon (3:24am GMT), South Vietnamese President Duong Van Minh announced the surrender of the nation to North Vietnamese invaders. "I believe firmly in reconciliation among Vietnamese to avoid unnecessary shedding of the blood of Vietnamese," said Minh. "For this reason, I ask the soldiers of the Republic of Vietnam to cease hostilities in calm and to stay where they are." The Viet Cong flag was raised over the presidential palace at 12:15. Earlier in the day, U.S. Ambassador Graham Martin was the last American diplomat to leave Saigon, lifting off of the U.S. Embassy roof at 4:58 am, and at 7:52 am USMC Colonel James Kean and ten U.S. Marines boarded an American helicopter and left, ending the U.S. presence in Vietnam. Saigon was renamed "Ho Chi Minh City"; Huynh Tan Phát of North Vietnam then administered the "Provisional Revolutionary Government of the Republic of South Vietnam" as President until July 2, 1976, when the area was formally incorporated by the North as part of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam
