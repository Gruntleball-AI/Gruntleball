wOBA Batting Leaders

This season, I'm doing things a little differently for the batter of the week. Instead of using Runs Created (RC) and saving myself a lot of work, I'm using Tom Tango's wOBA (weighted on-base average) to determine who is the best hitter each week. Derived from wOBA will be runs above average, which will determine who had the best overall week.

St Paul's Carl Yastrzemski led the league with a .537 wOBA and 5.9 wRAA with a batting line of 400/516/880 in 31 plate appearances. Runners-up were Cape Canaveral's Tony Perez (.501/5.4), Cary's Willie Horton (.587/5.2) and Cape Canaveral's Mark Belanger (.508/4.8). Bad hitter of the week goes to St Paul's Johnny Callison who cost his team six runs with a .059 wOBA.

wOBA Pitching Leaders

This season, I'm doing things a little differently for the pitchers of the week. Instead of using a blend of RCERA and CERA to determine RSAA (runs saved above average), to correspond with the batters, I'm using wOBA and wRAA to determine the best of the week.

On with the show... Cary's Luke Walker pitched 17.0 far stronger innings than his runs allowed would suggest, holding hitters to a .186 wOBA (against a league average of .336), resulting in eight runs saved above average (had everything broken more or less normally). Runners-up were Sophia's Tommy John with a .204 wOBA and 7.0 wRAA; Pakenham's Sonny Siebert with a .221 wOBA and 6.6 wRAA; Okinawa's Fergie Jenkins (.238/6.1); and Quincy's Bob Moose (.144/6.1). Reliever of the week was Ringling's Skip Lockwood saved 3.2 runs with a .214 wOBA. Bad pitcher of the week goes to Northfield's Steve Carlton who cost his team 8.8 runs with a .501 wOBA.
